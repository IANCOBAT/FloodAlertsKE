About this map's coverage

Nairobi's 60 areas follow the 2026 Nairobi Rivers Regeneration Programme mapping (five river corridors) plus independent flood-exposure research on Kibera, Mathare, Mukuru and Kariobangi. The remaining 32 areas draw on Kenya Meteorological Department advisories and disaster reporting from the March–May 2024 and March–May 2026 nationwide flood events, covering chronic hotspots (Budalangi, the Kano Plains, the Tana River basin, Garissa) and the 30 counties flagged during the 2026 floods. Rainfall is fetched live per area; historical flood-extent points on the map are Nairobi-only for now. None of this is calibrated hydrological modelling — see "Next production work" in the README.

This is decision support, not an emergency service. In an active emergency, follow instructions from KMD, your county government and national emergency authorities — or use the emergency button on the map.

If you're caught in flooding:
  • Move to higher ground as soon as water starts rising near you — don't wait to see how high it will get.
  • Never walk or drive through moving water; even shallow fast-moving water can sweep away a person or a vehicle.
  • Stay well away from downed power lines and submerged electrical equipment.
  • If trapped inside a building, move to the highest level and signal for help; avoid sealed attics where rising water could trap you.
  • Keep a charged phone, torch, important documents and a small emergency kit ready during rainy seasons.
  • After water recedes, avoid contact with floodwater where you can, and boil or treat drinking water before use.
